Reflection

My take on the graded run of 2026-10-14. Every claim points at evidence on this page: the 101, 102 and 103 tabs (Expected vs Actual, tool tables, screenshots) and the Cash tab. The raw steps are in output/audit_trail.json, entries #99 onward.

1 · How I’d grade the agents on each ticket, and why

#101 Bulldog tee · A− What went right.
  • The numbers were right: S at 0 on hand, invoice #501 at $840 and 3 days overdue against the shop’s today (2026-08-31), Bulldog Print Co at 5 lead days.
  • It saw what the invoice was. #501 is the rush reprint of this exact tee, so it prepared one payment instead of inventing a new purchase order.
  • The planned handoff happened: Inventory → Accounting.
  • Customer Service kept the shop’s business private. Draft #1 never mentions the $840 or the printer problem.
Why not an A:
  • The Boss also asked Accounting directly, in parallel, so Accounting ran twice (6 model calls, get_invoice_status and get_ticket twice each). Only the duplicate-payment guard in prepare_payment stopped a second $840 request.
  • It was the most expensive ticket: 21 model calls and 58,078 input tokens.
  • The draft is a bit vague. It says “we can’t promise a date”, when it could have said “about 5 days after the reprint is released”, which the tools did return.
#102 Rent due · A
  • It did the one thing a careful owner would do: checked Elm City Properties’ email against lease #1 before paying ($2,400, due 2026-09-02, exactly 2 days out).
  • Only two specialists, no wasted work. It was the cheapest run: 14 calls, 37,653 tokens, 89% cached.
  • The cash came out right: $2,560 → $160 after my approval (Cash tab, row 2).
  • The only miss is that the Boss relayed Facilities’ facts to Accounting itself, instead of Facilities handing off directly. It cost nothing, but it isn’t the handoff I planned.
#103 Bulk hoodies · B+ Good calls:
  • Stock was right: 8 of 20 M on hand, short 12.
  • The discount kept a healthy margin: 10% off ($52.20 each) still leaves $30.20 a hoodie, $604 on the order.
  • It correctly ordered nothing. 12 × $22 = $264 would exceed the $160 left after 101 and 102.
  • A customer got a reply this time. The dry run had skipped Customer Service.
What dragged it down:
  • The Boss’s summary says 12 “could arrive as early as 2026-09-05 if ordered today” without saying the shop can’t pay for that order.
  • Draft #2 talks about our insides: “Accounting has prepared a draft offer… cannot be presented as a final offer until a human approves it.” That’s honest, but a student club doesn’t need our org chart.
  • No one offered the real alternative sitting on the shelf: L has 14 (also S 4, XL 6). The club could have walked away with 20 hoodies today, 8 M + 12 L.
Overall
  • Money: zero cash mistakes. Two payments, both signed by a human, and the ending $160.00 matches cash_accounts to the cent (Cash tab).
  • Facts: no invented numbers. Every figure in every report traces to an MCP tool result.
  • Weak spots: coordination (an extra call on 101, missing handoffs on 102 and 103) and judgment in customer wording (103), not accuracy.
  • Cost of all three runs: 54 model calls, 148,506 input tokens (87% cached) and 5,236 output tokens on gpt-6-luna.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

  • #102 is the clearest case. One agent with get_lease_status(1) → prepare_payment("rent", 1, 102) → update_ticket_status does the whole job in about 3 tool calls and 4 model calls. The team needed 14 model calls and 37,653 input tokens, because the Boss alone made 8 of them just to read, delegate and wrap up.
  • #103 is almost as clear. check_stock("CC-HOOD-NAVY","M",20) already returns stock, list price, cost and margin in one call. One agent could then call prepare_price_quote and draft_customer_message and be done. Instead, Inventory and Accounting both called check_stock and find_vendors for the same 20 hoodies.
  • Every specialist re-reads the ticket. get_ticket ran 15 times across three runs (6 on #101 alone). That’s the tax of handing work between separate agents that don’t share context.
  • Why one agent would be simpler here:
    • The tickets are small and the hard parts (overdue days, shortfalls, margins, “no negative cash”, “vendor won’t ship with an open invoice”) are already enforced inside the MCP tools in code, so the specialists add little reasoning the tools don’t already do.
    • Fewer handoffs also means fewer coordination failures: the duplicate Accounting call on 101 and the missing handoffs on 102 and 103 can’t happen with one agent.
  • Where the team still earns its keep:
    • Least privilege. Customer Service physically cannot prepare a payment, and the Boss cannot write a quote; that is enforced in agents/mcp.py.
    • A readable story on the board. You can see who did what.
    For three tickets that’s a nice-to-have. For a real shop with dozens of tickets a day, many people, and money moving, the separation and the audit trail start to matter more than the token bill.

  1. The Crest Mug is sold out. check_stock("CC-MUG-CREST","OS") shows 0.
    • Inventory → find_vendors("mugs") finds Elm City Gifts: 3 lead days, no open invoices, will_ship: true.
    • Accounting → prepare_purchase_order for 24 mugs at $3.50 = $84.00. That fits inside today’s $160, unlike the hoodie restock, so a human can approve it, and the mugs arrive around 2026-09-03.
    • The tool computes the cost from pricing and checks the vendor’s invoices, so nothing is guessed.
    • Same path as #101: Inventory finds the vendor and Accounting prepares the money. The difference is that this vendor isn’t blocked by an unpaid bill the way Bulldog Print Co was by #501.
  2. An orientation group wants 30 Yale Caps at a discount.
    • check_stock("CC-HAT-BLUE","OS",30) shows 40 on hand, so it can be filled today.
    • Accounting → prepare_price_quote(…, 30, 15) gives $18.70 each with a $12.70 margin. The tool would refuse anything at or below the $6 cost.
    • Customer Service drafts the offer, and a human clicks Confirm quote. That’s the exact path #103 took (Inventory → Accounting quote → Customer Service draft → human confirm), but with stock on the shelf, so nothing is short.
  3. October rent is coming and the till is nearly empty. After paying, lease #1’s next_due is now 2026-10-02 and checking is $160.
    • When Elm City Properties emails again, Facilities confirms the $2,400, and Accounting prepares it. balance_if_all_pending_approved then shows −$2,240 immediately.
    • If someone clicks approve anyway, approve_payment_request refuses (“balances never go negative”).
    • The team can’t create the money, but it flags the shortfall weeks early and makes sure no one bounces a rent check.
    • Built straight on #102 and the Cash tab: my #102 approval is what moved next_due to 2026-10-02, and the $160 left is the Cash tab’s ending balance.

5 · Three new problems it could not solve, and what I’d add

  1. Selling anything, and getting paid.
    • The gap: the Yale AI Club accepts the quote and pays $417.60 for the 8 M hoodies on hand. Nothing can take 8 off inventory (it still says M = 8 after the run) or add cash. In this build cash only goes out, which is exactly why the till ended at $160 with no way back up.
    • Tools to add: record_sale(ticket_id, sku, size, qty, unit_price), which lowers inventory, writes a sales table and raises cash_accounts; and a human-gated issue_refund.
    • Agent to add: a Sales / Orders agent, or a write-enabled Inventory, plus a cash-flow forecast tool for Accounting, so “can we afford October rent?” has a real answer.
    • Where I hit this: #103 ended with a confirmed $1,044 quote and $0 of income, and the Cash tab can only ever go down.
  2. The reprint arrives, and time moves on.
    • The gap: Bulldog Print Co ships the S tees around 2026-09-05, but no tool can receive stock, and desk.date_today is frozen at 2026-08-31. So ticket 101 can never truly close with “your tee is ready”, and nothing notices when a lead time has passed.
    • Tools to add: receive_shipment(purchase_order_id | invoice_id, qty), which raises inventory; advance_shop_day() (human-run); and a list_due_items(days) watcher.
    • Agent to add: a scheduled Receiving / Follow-up agent that wakes up on lead-day dates, updates stock, and asks Customer Service to draft the “it’s here!” message to Tauhid Zaman.
    • Where I hit this: #101 is “resolved” because the $840 is paid, but Tauhid still has no tee. S is still 0 in inventory.
  3. Real conversations and disputes.
    • The gap: Bulldog Print Co replies “we never got the $840”, or Tauhid writes back “actually, M is fine, ship it today.” The team has no inbox, no way to send, and no record of the payment ever leaving the bank. A payments row is all it knows.
